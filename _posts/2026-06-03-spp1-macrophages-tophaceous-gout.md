## Reference

This note is based on a journal club presentation of the following study:

**Deconstruction of tophi and synovium defines SPP1+ macrophages involved in extracellular matrix remodelling in gout.** *Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases*. 2025. PMID: 41107120. DOI: [10.1016/j.ard.2025.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.09.003).

## Central Question

The classical framework of gout explains acute flares through monosodium urate (MSU) crystal deposition, macrophage phagocytosis, NLRP3 inflammasome activation, caspase-1 cleavage, IL-1 beta release, and pyroptotic inflammation. This model is powerful for understanding acute gouty arthritis, but it is less sufficient for explaining chronic tophus formation, spatially restricted joint destruction, fibrosis, and extracellular matrix remodeling.

The conceptual question of this paper is therefore:

> How does persistent MSU deposition become organized into a chronic, spatially structured inflammatory niche that drives tophus maturation and joint damage?

## Background Logic

The presentation places this study within a progressive research trajectory. Earlier work emphasized that hyperuricaemia and urate transporters such as SLC2A9, SLC22A12, and ABCG2 regulate systemic urate burden, while local MSU crystals trigger acute macrophage-mediated inflammation. Subsequent studies began to shift attention from systemic urate alone to the local joint microenvironment.

The literature sequence can be understood as four steps:

1. Cartilage and extracellular matrix components can influence MSU crystallization and inflammatory cell recruitment.
2. Damaged joints may provide a permissive local microenvironment for gout localization and recurrence.
3. Imaging and organoid-based models can visualize the spatiotemporal deposition of MSU crystals.
4. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics can define the immune-stromal architecture of established tophi.

The 2025 study extends this line of reasoning by moving from "crystal-triggered inflammation" to "spatially organized tissue remodeling."

## Study Design

The study integrates single-cell transcriptomic and spatial approaches to deconstruct the cellular architecture of tophi and synovial tissue. The presentation highlights a dataset of 44,221 cells and downstream analyses involving cell annotation, trajectory inference, enrichment analysis, spatial mapping, and cell-cell communication.

The key analytic goal is not only to identify which cell types are present, but also to determine how disease-associated macrophage states interact with fibroblasts, osteoblast-lineage cells, osteoclast-lineage cells, and regulatory T cells within different topographic regions of the tophus.

## Main Findings

### 1. Tophaceous gout contains a disease-associated macrophage state

The core cellular discovery is a population of **SPP1+ tophaceous gout-associated macrophages**, abbreviated as TGAMs. These cells are characterized by a transcriptional program involving markers such as **SPP1**, **MMP9**, and **CHI3L1**.

This macrophage state is distinct from macrophage populations associated with intercritical gout or acute synovial inflammation. Functionally, TGAMs appear to represent a chronic inflammatory and tissue-remodeling macrophage program rather than a purely acute inflammasome-driven state.

### 2. Cell-cell communication is stronger in tophaceous gout

Compared with intercritical gout, tophaceous gout shows markedly enhanced intercellular communication. The dominant axis highlighted in the presentation is the **SPP1-CD44 interaction**, which links SPP1+ TGAMs with fibroblasts and osteoblast-lineage cells.

This is important because it connects macrophage activation to stromal remodeling and bone-related cellular programs. In this model, macrophages are not simply inflammatory responders; they become organizers of the local tissue niche.

### 3. The tophus is spatially organized

A major conceptual contribution is the spatial separation of signaling programs. The presentation describes **SPP1 signaling** as predominant in the corona zone, whereas **collagen signaling** is more prominent in the fibrovascular zone.

This suggests that the tophus is not a homogeneous inflammatory mass. Instead, it contains layered microdomains: an inner immune-stromal interaction zone enriched for SPP1-driven communication and an outer structural remodeling zone enriched for collagen-associated matrix organization.

### 4. Chronic inflammation is coupled to ECM remodeling

The SPP1+ TGAM program is linked to extracellular matrix remodeling, fibrosis, osteoclast differentiation, and structural joint damage. This provides a mechanistic bridge between persistent urate crystal deposition and the irreversible tissue changes seen in chronic tophaceous gout.

In this sense, SPP1+ macrophages can be interpreted as a cellular connector between immune activation and anatomical remodeling.

## Proposed Disease Model

The logic of the study can be summarized as the following sequence:

```text
Persistent hyperuricaemia
        ↓
MSU crystal deposition in joints
        ↓
Formation of a tophus-specific microenvironment
        ↓
Expansion of SPP1+ TGAMs
        ↓
Activation of SPP1-CD44 signaling
        ↓
Crosstalk with fibroblasts, osteoblast-lineage cells,
osteoclast-lineage cells, and regulatory T cells
        ↓
ECM remodeling, fibrosis, and osteoclast differentiation
        ↓
Tophus maturation and joint damage
```

This model reframes tophaceous gout as a chronic multicellular remodeling process rather than only a consequence of crystal accumulation.

## Interpretation

The most important conceptual advance is the transition from a linear inflammatory model to a spatial tissue-ecology model. In acute gout, the central axis is MSU crystals activating macrophage inflammasomes. In chronic tophaceous gout, the central axis becomes the interaction between macrophages, stromal cells, matrix remodeling, and bone destruction within a structured lesion.

SPP1+ TGAMs are therefore not merely a marker-defined cluster. They represent a candidate organizing cell state that may coordinate chronic inflammation, fibrosis, and tissue remodeling through SPP1-CD44 signaling.

## Critical Points

Several questions remain important for interpretation:

1. Whether SPP1+ TGAMs are drivers of tophus progression or a consequence of the established tophus microenvironment.
2. Whether targeting SPP1-CD44 signaling would reduce tissue remodeling without impairing necessary resolution or repair programs.
3. How reversible the TGAM-associated niche is after effective urate-lowering therapy.
4. Whether similar macrophage-stromal remodeling axes operate in other crystal-associated or chronic inflammatory joint diseases.

## Take-Home Message

This study provides a spatial and single-cell framework for understanding chronic tophaceous gout. The main message is that persistent MSU deposition can induce a structured tissue niche in which SPP1+ macrophages communicate with stromal and bone-lineage cells, coupling chronic inflammation to extracellular matrix remodeling, fibrosis, and joint destruction.

For future work, this framework suggests that gout research should integrate systemic urate genetics, local crystal biology, macrophage state transitions, and spatial tissue remodeling into a unified disease model.
